# Pineapple Siege architecture

The browser runs a flight and destruction sandbox on a seeded 9,216 m island. The main thread owns input, menus, rendering, audio, and persistence. A simulation worker owns flight, combat, damage, terrain edits, civilians, monsters, and Rapier physics. A separate generation worker builds island candidates.

## Application

`src/main.ts` boots `src/app/game.ts`. The session state in `src/session-state.ts` distinguishes loading, menu, playing, photo transitions, photo mode, island preview, and recovery. Application modules own input, settings, HUD, island selection, and incremental saving. Pause and photo mode freeze simulation and audio. Graphics recovery rebuilds renderer resources before resuming.

`src/app/island-coordinator.ts` handles preview, reroll, seed sharing, and replacement. `src/app/save-coordinator.ts` handles persistence and protected-save recovery. IndexedDB stores the exact generated baseline separately from damage; replacement writes the baseline and removes prior damage atomically. Failed writes, historical saves, and temporary play retain their recovery protections.

## Simulation

`src/sim/simulation.ts` coordinates the original tick order. `weapon-driver.ts` owns firing and projectile handling; `destruction-driver.ts` owns queued destruction orchestration. Spatial queries, incremental destruction work, terrain streaming, bounded physics/cosmetic pools, and wreckage cleanup support large worlds. Packed transferable snapshots are recycled and interpolated by the main thread.

The portal to hell is a giant toilet (`src/realm.ts`). Each realm appends it at runtime from the island's portal anchor, turned and nudged off buildings. Twelve retired slots from the old stone ring come first, then one block per owner in `src/world/toilet-manifest.json`. Island, world and save versions are unchanged. Aircraft diving through the seat hole skip collision down to the bowl's water plane, where the realm switch fires. Breaking any bowl or seat owner closes the portal. Toilet blocks use the `porcelain` material, whose debris renders as curved glazed shards with a spray of cosmetic chips. `ToiletView` draws the exported mesh and removes owner index ranges, as the approved god does; `PortalView` draws only the swirling water. See [the toilet asset](public/assets/toilet/README.md).

The pineapple god is a textured throne model (`pineapple-god/source.glb`), packaged by `scripts/export-pineapple-god.mjs` onto the monument's 815 legacy owner slots by role. `ApprovedGodView` draws it with the model's own textures; `applyApprovedGod` refits cached monuments' colliders and foundations when the manifest revision changes. A broken owner splits into at most 8 aligned halvings of its box, cut into geometry within a 3 ms per-frame budget; each body carries its cell (packed beside the roof-wedge tag), and `GodDebris` draws flying pieces as one batched mesh, and settled pieces as one merged mesh per rubble batch, both cut from the owner's own textured triangles. `solid.bin` fills the shell with owner-labelled 5 m voxels: `GodWounds` caps broken owners' neighbours with rough stone, and each piece carries the stone of its own voxels, its corners jittered identically so pieces match their wounds. Hell's effigy owners keep plain blocks. See [the god asset](public/assets/pineapple-god/README.md).

The player can swap the aircraft for a monster concrete truck (V). `PlaneState.mode` is `"ground"` while driving, so monsters, flies, civilians, weapons and saves keep treating it as the player. Swapping down sets `chute`: the truck parachutes from the aircraft's altitude onto a parking spot clear of structures, water, burning fuel and monsters. Swapping up sets `launch`, a short nose-up rocket climb. `Simulation.drive` runs arcade handling over the authoritative terrain. The horizontal part of `PlaneState.v` carries momentum: keys steer through an eased, speed-limited bicycle model, and tyre grip scrubs sideways speed, so the tail slides out on the jets, on water or rubble, and after crooked landings. The truck rides on the average of its four wheel contacts, which smooths the 2 m terrain facets. Slopes pull it along the ground, and speed is kept along the surface. It leaves the ground only when the ground falls away faster than gravity can follow (`stickFactor`), from a lip when the rear axle clears it. Landings keep the speed along the surface they land on. The sim also springs the body's pitch, roll and landing heave, and publishes `steer`, `slip` and `heave` for the renderer. Turning in place needs the throttle, and the truck hops when pinned. While driving, the mouse aims a roof turret instead of steering (`InputState.aimYaw/aimPitch`, published as `PlaneState.aimYaw/aimPitch`), and every weapon fires along that aim. The chase camera follows the aim, so the crosshair matches it. `TRUCK.scale` sizes the model and its collision together. Its sweep is structure-only and never sticks on an initial overlap, so the truck rolls over terrain and rubble, shoves loose debris and flattens trees. Fast impacts and deep water wreck it. A `shield` after respawns and swaps ignores fire, monsters, flies and lasers. Nukes and gravity bombs are lobbed forward. Hell stays flight-only. On the render side, `concrete-truck.ts` builds the mixer, `vehicle-transition.ts` plays the smoke-puff, parachute and rocket swap and blends the chase camera, and `concrete-pour.ts` streams cosmetic concrete into unsaved puddles while F is held.

Live weapon configuration uses fixed defaults plus rapid fire. Save-boundary parsers retain historical profiles and pending-strike parameters. Monsters still interrupt attacks and dance during laser strikes. Civilian casualties, morale, and celebrations remain simulation behavior.

## Rendering and audio

`src/render/renderer.ts` coordinates world batches, actors, effects, environment, terrain, and cameras. Subsystems own update/reset/dispose behavior. A shared disposal collector releases shared resources once. World batches retain generated entity identity and destruction ownership; decoration never changes collision or support data.

Shared standard materials distinguish stone, wood, roofs, terrain, and foliage. Trees share species geometry, baked crown shading and one foliage clock across near/distant draws and shadows; mills and banners are static. Coastal beaches and rocks follow terrain revisions, while surf and water use the authoritative height and flood textures. Ocean and rivers share authoritative terrain/flood masks. Rivers use a flow-mapped variant of the water material: each vertex carries its downstream direction and a `current` (speed, white water) from the grade averaged over ±20 m, two noise layers half a cycle apart scroll downstream and cross-fade, depth above the bed tints shallows and foams the banks, and falls draw as curved sheets with churned water below. PS7 rivers follow a centripetal Catmull-Rom curve through the drainage nodes with a hashed meander that fades at sources, joins and steep reaches; `riverChannelHeight` carves a rounded bed that shelves to 0.3 m at the half-width, and `riverBankIndex` lets terrain shading add wet mud, pebble shoulders and lusher grass along the banks. Lighting uses a procedural sky, ambient light, one directional light, bounded shadows, emissive windows, and aerial haze. `shadow-frame.ts` sizes the sun shadow box in discrete altitude steps capped by the quality profile, leads it along the view, snaps it to shadow texels, scales bias with texel size, and widens terrain and batch caster radii only when the box grows. `aerial-fog.ts` patches the shared fog chunks once so linear `Fog` evaluates exponential height fog (Hell keeps `FogExp2`); the ocean backdrop applies the same integral and the ocean fades scene fog across its blend to avoid double haze. Additive effect materials opt out of fog. `terrain-sun-mask.ts` builds a 512² caster grid from terrain heights and intact statue parts (sampled 64 rows per frame), asks `terrain-sun-mask-worker.ts` for a horizon-shadow mask whenever the light turns half a degree or casters change, and scales direct diffuse and specular light by it in every installed standard material except water; `EnvironmentView.installMaterial` applies it alongside the disco projection. `sky-bodies.ts` supplies the sun disc and glow, the moon (opposite the sun, swung 0.45 rad; phase from the sun direction) and hashed stars to the sky shader; `EnvironmentLighting` hands the directional light from sun to moon below the horizon. `sky-reflection.ts` adds an analytic zenith/horizon/ground reflection to metallic materials without an envMap. `dynamic-lights.ts` patches installed standard materials with a loop over 8 uniform light slots (view-space position and range, colour × intensity) that stops at a uniform count, so lights come and go without the shader recompiles Three point lights would cause. Each frame the renderer adds explosion flashes, fire patches, building fires, burning fuel, muzzle flashes and a falling aircraft as candidates; the brightest in-view ones fill the quality profile's slots and fires also draw additive halo sprites. `window-glow.ts` draws one additive sprite per intact window at night. Presentation supports antialiasing, photo focus, and an optional display-space CRT shader after output conversion. CRT presets (Off, Subtle, Retro TV) affect the canvas and exported photos, while HTML controls stay crisp. The pass reuses composer targets, tracks drawing-buffer resolution, and records `crtSubmit` timing. Subtle is the default; CRT selection is independent of reduced effects and auto quality. Reflection targets, AO, bloom, and heat distortion are removed.

Audio uses one engine loop, one ambient bed, and shared positional cues with voice limits. Volume, mute, pause, reset, and disposal apply to all voices. Runtime audio hashes are generated and verified during builds; source attribution is in `public/assets/audio/CREDITS.md`.

## Compatibility and builds

Play requires revision 7 / world version 13 (`playableIsland`, `parsePlayableIslandCode`). PS4, PS5 and PS6 generation and validation are retained for tests and tooling only; a saved island from an older generator is replaced automatically on load, and older codes are rejected. Save compatibility stays at version 10, with optional exact-index sidecars for far-grid terrain. Preference revision 5 adds the CRT selection, defaults missing or invalid modes to Subtle, and preserves remaining choices. `src/world/generator.mjs` is shared by build tooling and the generation worker. The worker plans and materializes one seed-driven island per request; replacing the worker cancels obsolete work. Validation checks coastlines, inland water, population, graded roads, flight corridors, budgets, identities, and grounded supports before acceptance. See [island generation](docs/ISLAND_GENERATION.md).

Vite builds the game and an isolated `performance.html` harness. Terrain parts remain below Cloudflare Pages' asset-size limit; the unsplit test terrain is omitted from production. Generated world files, runtime hashes, build output, and benchmark artifacts are ignored. The lockfile pins dependency installation.
